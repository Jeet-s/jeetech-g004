# Nonogram Atelier — Level Plan

120 handcrafted pictures on 10 islands × 12. Every picture is a recognisable drawing, proven by
`npm run validate` to have exactly one solution reachable by line logic alone (no guessing).
Each island introduces **one** mechanic (or twists an earlier one), practises it, combines it with what came
before, and ends with a **Masterwork** that renovates a room of the atelier.

## How difficulty is built (and measured)

Nonogram difficulty is not grid size — it is how *scarce* the next deduction is. The solver
(`logicSolve` in `src/game/nonogram.ts`) solves best-first, one line at a time, and records:

- **looks** – productive line steps needed;
- **passes** – rounds of "look at every line once" (logic depth: how often a row must wait for columns);
- **open** – how many lines yield anything at the start (many = gentle entry, few = a hard first foothold);
- **search** – Σ (open lines ÷ productive lines) over the solve: the expected number of lines a player
  must read to find each deduction. `npm run validate` reports difficulty = search × √passes × colour/hidden-clue
  factors × the casual bots' first-try stall rate (a deep board with an obvious next step is long, not hard).

Bots (`src/game/sim.ts`) play the same engine: a look at a line that yields advances the board; a casual
player sometimes reads a dead-end line that already holds their own marks and inks on a hunch, which ends the first try (the real game never
punishes it — free undo, no strikes). Boards where deductions are scarce therefore fail more first tries,
for the right reason. `scripts/tune-levels.ts` sets each level's "tempting lines" to follow the curve: breathers after a boss sit ~10 points
above the trend, a new mechanic's teach levels 3-8 points above it, bosses only ~4 points below it, nothing below ~52% casual, and
skilled players winning ≥ 90% of every level (at least 8 points ahead of casual) — a skilled loss rate above ~10% would mean the
board hides a guess. (Each knob also carries a scan
order — a rotation of the moves list that never changes a level's true win rate, only aligns the factory bots' short-cycle RNG.)

## Late-game curve (islands 7-10)

Each late island is **two smooth climbs** (round-7 review, 2026-10-29: the old mid-island valleys at slots 4 and 10 made
every practise board between them read as an "isolated spike" — 57% among 70-80% neighbours). Climb one runs from the
teach (slot 1, ~76% casual) through 70 / 66 / 62 / 58% to the boss (slot 6, ~55%); climb two runs from the breather
(slot 7, ~80%+, a small quick board) through 68 / 64 / 60 / 56% to the Masterwork (slot 12, ~51%), the island's
hardest board. Every step is ~4 points harder than the one before it, so difficulty never dips and spikes back.
Skilled players win ~85-95% throughout, so no board hides a guess. Mean casual ≈ 64% (QA gate ≤ 65%).
In islands 1-6 the Masterwork (slot 12) likewise sits clearly below its island's boss (slot 6).
Every new-mechanic combination gets a small bridge first: void + colour (L45),
smudged + colour (L74), cracked + colour (L87), tally + colour (L97), tally + void / smudged (Grand Exhibit).

## Openers (no single trick)

The first deduction differs from board to board, so the set never reads as "fill the full row, then the columns":
- **exact fit with no full line** — L2 (row 4's 4 3 is the only exact line), L10 (row 4's 3 3 3 crosses column 6's 1 9);
- **overlap on a split clue** — L5 Seahorse (column 4's 2 5 2 is 11 in twelve: all three parts overlap), L6 (row 3's 3 5 still covers columns 8-9);
- **spare-cell overlap** — L9 Anchor (the stock and the shaft overlap; the ring's sides follow), L11 (rows 7-8's "5 3" and "4 3" both cover column 4, which spends its 2 and walls the rest);
- **counting (tally)** — island 2: a ringed number is met by its crossings (L13), or has exactly that many cells left (L14), on the perch (L15), on the quills (L16), interlocking on the deer (L18), mid-board on the heron's leg (L24);
- **coloured counting** — island 9: one count per colour, in no order (L97-L108);
- **reading the colour edge from the columns** — L28 (the watermelon rind);
- **colours that must touch** — L25 (row 3's red 2, green 3, red 2 only fits seven cells if the colours touch);
- **two exact rows hand over a hidden column** — L81; **two hidden rows at once** — L82;
- **a stamp that settles an ambiguity** — L56 (the toucan's beak could shift one column; the stamped tile fixes it), L59 (the swan's rows 9-10 could swap ends);
- **a stamp where two hidden lines cross** — L113;
- **a void inside the canvas** — L40; **a void forcing a run** — L38, L45, L47;
- **a gap-forcing column** — L34 (column 9's 1 1 1 1 drip line);
- **reading a hidden row from its columns, one by one** — L73, L74, L79.
The "lone 1 is the pole, so X the rest" step is never a level's aha (it survives only as a minor step in L9).

## Pacing pattern (every island)

| slot | role | purpose |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | teach | the island's new mechanic **alone**, on a small, friendly board |
| 2 | teach | the same idea once more, from a different angle |
| 3 | practise | the mechanic on a fuller picture |
| 4 | practise | the mechanic with an earlier technique |
| 5 | twist | the mechanic used in a surprising way |
| 6 | **boss** | mid-island spike: twist + earlier mechanics, tighter logic |
| 7 | breather | a small, quick comfort picture after the boss (about half the boss's looks) — one cute use of the mechanic |
| 8 | practise | back to rhythm on a bigger canvas |
| 9 | combine | the new mechanic with two earlier ones |
| 10 | twist | the hardest single idea of the island |
| 11 | combine | long-form combination, sets up the finale |
| 12 | **finale** | Masterwork — biggest board of the island, tests mastery; renovates a room |

Target casual first-try win rate: ~97% on levels 1-2, then 93% / 90% / 87% on L3-L5 (the opening is not flat); every teach
level ≥ ~83%, every breather ~89-93% early and ~80%+ late; then sliding from ~90% to ~60% across the campaign — first third
~90→80%, middle third ~80→70%, last third two smooth climbs per island (see "Late-game curve"). The Masterwork (slot 12) is
always the island's hardest board, below the boss (slot 6); skilled bots win ≥ ~85-90% everywhere. (The GDD's "spike at puzzle 10 of islands 3, 5, 7, 9" maps onto the boss slots of this 12-level structure; "every
5th puzzle relaxed" maps onto the slot-7 breathers.)
